01 / CausalTrace

Preserve the corpus before interpreting it.

CausalTrace freezes saved transcripts, joins them to benchmark questions by stable IDs, checks answer extraction, hashes its inputs, and creates a stratified annotation sample. A failed early import parsed zero answers; a repaired import parsed all 100 smoke-test records. That was a software repair, rather than a change in model ability.

The full historical corpus contains 20,224 condition-specific records. Its 19,102 correct answers differ by one from the later Kimi archive’s 19,103. The snapshots are retained separately.

Correct answers can have weak support

Among 420 annotated correct-answer reasoning traces, 37 were marked as containing invalid reasoning and 29 lacked an answer-supporting valid argument. The weighted invalid-reasoning estimate was 9.14%. It applies to the selected eligible correct-answer population and the supplied annotations; a second annotator and adjudication remained pending.

A dataset that makes meaningful changes visible

The generated contrast release has 3,456 items: 3,072 with explicit, scored mechanisms and 384 deliberately ambiguous diagnostics. Its 1,536 AND/OR pairs are evenly split between cases where a rule change should flip the answer and cases where it should preserve it.

Paraphrases and premise order changes keep the structural problem fixed. Replacing AND with OR changes the mechanism. Ambiguous cases retain both interpretations and are excluded from official accuracy scoring. The 160-item pilot contains 128 scored items and 32 ambiguous diagnostics. No completed model score set was located for these generated tasks.

02 / Mechanistic programme

Can internal signals explain an answer?

A separate exploratory programme uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and comparison models to inspect answer preferences across transformer layers. It tests probes, donor activation replacement, renaming, persona directions, an attempted neural do-operator, LiReF steering, ablations, and fine-tuning.

These findings are manuscript-reported. The audit located the thesis and paper materials, but not the experiment-level scripts, tensors, splits, and response records needed to reproduce the central claims. This is different from the raw-backed Qwen3 Boolean experiments.

The programme is exploratory, single-seed and unregistered. Seed 42 selects the core 399 questions as 133 per rung, with 600 questions per model in the comparison. Each response is scored from one forward pass using last-prompt-token residuals and yes/no logits; no chain of thought is generated in this protocol.

What the measurements mean

A logit comparison measures which of “yes” or “no” has the higher next-token score. A probe trains a separate classifier to read a label from a hidden state. Patching replaces a hidden activation with another example’s activation. Steering adds a chosen direction to that activation. These are different measurements and interventions.

“Commitment” is defined as the first layer after which the yes-minus-no preference retains its final sign with a minimum margin of 0.10. Early commitment means relative depth below 0.55. It is a depth-based diagnostic: early layers can already access every prompt token through attention.

What was retained

  • A full 10,112-item commitment census, and 600-item comparisons across base and instruction-tuned models.
  • 399-item experiments on probes, donor patching, trajectory-based error prediction, persona additions and base/instruct representations.
  • An 80-story signed activation intervention and 200-item intervention-rung test that moved in the wrong intended direction.
  • LiReF analysis of 9,000 questions and renamed variants, with selective repairs and harms that require separate denominators.
  • A 798-item anticommonsense test, direction/PCA ablations, and manuscript-reported QLoRA comparisons.
  • Subskill segment probes; a Corr2Cause transfer attempt invalidated by collapsed input segments; an unreported bridge proposal.

The thesis says fourteen experiments but lists fifteen labels after omitting H and J. Renaming and cross-validation descriptions also differ across sections. Missing raw evidence leaves those discrepancies unresolved.

03 / Reasoning assessment

A judge also needs to be checked.

Several projects asked another language model to label mistakes in explanations. Their completed outputs, failed attempts, and reference comparisons are distinct experimental units.

A later local GPT-OSS campaign submitted 23,771 trace packets. Its schema checks passed, but exact-evidence, contradiction, and reference-agreement checks failed. High reported confidence did not reliably distinguish correct from incorrect judgments. Its error rates therefore describe the judge’s labels, rather than a validated census of reasoning failures.

One set of reference labels came from model adjudication, not human gold. Elsewhere, three assistant reviewers agreed on many selected examples, but that is still agent agreement. A Jinesis human handoff contained 240 analysis items plus a separate 20-item pilot; all eight human-label fields remained blank.

These efforts are useful for finding suspicious traces and designing examples. They do not supply completed independent human validation for the whole archive.

04 / Adjacent intervention research

Changing model behaviour is a different causal question.

The archive also contains work on personas and personalization. Here “causal” refers to changing an input or activation and measuring the response; it does not mean the model solved a causal-inference problem.

Persona steering and controls

A Llama-3-8B-Instruct activation study swept coefficients 4, 8 and 16 at layer 13 across 14 personas. Its 196 aggregate rows represent 3,920 generations. Prompting gave the strongest selected keyword score among the listed conditions. Keyword scores and coherence are proxies for style, rather than causal-question accuracy or human-rated personality adoption.

In an 8,400-record persona classification dataset, the layer-4 activation classifier reached 0.925357 accuracy. Improving the text-only control raised its score from 0.805595 to 0.919762 and then 0.925357. The final text control matched the activation result, weakening a claim of additional information unique to the internal representation.

A separate angular-versus-additive study retained small lexical changes across Gemma, Llama and Qwen. Its limited proxy results do not show a general solution to reliable internal control.

Personalization experiments

A completed targeted run contains 1,200 generations using Llama3.1:8B. It changes profile matching, numerical versus instruction representations, narration, response prefixes, and relevant versus irrelevant trait edits. A separate N=200 human-study reanalysis found no detected improvement in feeling heard.

Four exploratory pilots produced 2,816 replies: preference/history matching (800), emotional/interpersonal/neutral framing (960), delegated choice (672), and permission/disclosure (384). All 16 planned primary pilot comparisons had adjusted intervals containing zero. Seeds and repeated orders are repeated measurements, not extra independent people or scenarios.

Later manuscripts describe a different 3,600-response component design. Its number cannot replace the 1,200-run or 2,816-pilot denominators. These adjacent studies do not increase the CLadder sample.

Earlier presentations are historical

An eight-slide “Causal Trap” presentation described 263 correct-expression/wrong-answer cases and framed the failures as occurring after extraction. Later benchmark, information and transplant audits narrow that interpretation. Those slide numbers are retained as historical claims on the results page.

Accessible summaries, manuscript bundles, repeated exports and delivery copies describe overlapping work. They are not independent replications. A pre-existing blog’s synthetic 90.00% Opus claim lacks a recovered denominator, while 9,881 structured GPT-labelled rows support its 92.87% stored score. The newer October campaign is a distinct evaluation.

06 / The boundary of the archive

Plans, gaps, and what is genuinely unfinished.

  • The October production run was still active in the supplied snapshot. Later completions are not available in these files.
  • CausalTrace contrast tasks were generated, but completed model robustness scores were not found.
  • A second annotation pass, adjudication, and the Jinesis human handoff remained unfinished.
  • Matched template ablations, multi-seed free-generation tests, additional base/instruct pairs, BoolQ/StrategyQA transfer, polarity predictors, and a subskill-to-conversation bridge were recorded as proposals.
  • A progress note claimed a final-question re-read habit fixed direction errors, but supplied no denominator or independently linked results. It remains a progress claim.
  • Missing neural experiment artifacts prevent reproduction of the manuscript’s internal-mechanism and training claims.

The audit inspected accessible project records and selected documents. It did not establish completeness for protected, locked, deleted, cloud-only, or unreadable material. Dependency keyword hits, duplicate bundles, browser-history pointers, and unrelated predictive projects do not contribute new causal reasoning results.

The website closes the available evidence into one navigable account. Its conclusion is bounded by that evidence.
